01 — Apex Structure

Every civic and infrastructure body in Bengaluru ultimately reports up to the Government of Karnataka, either directly through the Chief Minister's own portfolio or through a sectoral minister and department. 02 — Municipal Governance

Greater Bengaluru Authority (GBA)

The GBA replaced the Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) as the city's primary civic body under the Greater Bengaluru Governance Act, 2024, and has been operational since September 2025. It introduced a three-tier structure: the GBA itself at the apex, five new city corporations in the middle, and ward committees at the grassroots.

Ward committees sit at the base of the GBA structure — the grassroots tier meant to bring elected corporators and residents into local decisions on roads, sanitation and civic amenities. The first elections to the GBA corporations are scheduled for 2026, following the transition from BBMP.

  2. Zonal CommissionersOne per zone (2 per corporation)
  3. Assistant Revenue Officers & Executive EngineersZone-level tax collection & civic works

  5. Elected Corporators & Ward CommitteesGrassroots representation once GBA elections are held

05 — How It Fits Together

Reading the map

Two parallel tracks

GBA and its five corporations handle place-based, day-to-day civic services — roads, waste, drains, property tax. The parastatal agencies (BWSSB, BESCOM, BMRCL, BMTC, BDA) each handle one sector city-wide, independent of GBA's ward and zone boundaries. A single street can involve GBA for the footpath, BWSSB for the water line beneath it, BESCOM for the streetlight, and BMRCL for the metro pillar next to it.

Coordination points

DULT/UMTA is the formal forum meant to align BMTC, BMRCL, BDA, GBA and the Traffic Police on transport projects. The Lokayukta and KSPCB sit outside this operating hierarchy as independent oversight bodies — the former on integrity and conduct, the latter on environmental compliance — with jurisdiction over every agency above.

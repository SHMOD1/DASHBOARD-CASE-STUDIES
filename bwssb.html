Bengaluru Water Supply & Sewerage Board

Bengaluru Water Supply & Sewerage Board (BWSSB)

Supplies Cauvery and groundwater to Bengaluru and treats the city's sewage — a state statutory board reporting to the Urban Development Department.

Formed
1964 (BWSSB Act)
Reports to
Bengaluru Development portfolio — Krishna Byre Gowda
Accountability chain

Who answers to whom

A classic three-wing utility structure: political Chairperson, an appointed Board, and an engineering hierarchy running from Engineer-in-Chief down to the sub-division.

Governing board / council

The seven-member Board

Per the BWSSB Act, 1964

  • Chairperson, appointed by the State Government
  • Seven Members appointed by the State Government, drawn from engineering, commercial/administrative and accounting/financial backgrounds
  • A Financial Adviser & Secretary sits alongside the Board for financial administration
Major Projects

What it's currently building or running

BWSSB's current flagship programme is bringing Cauvery water and sewerage to the 110 villages added to Bengaluru's boundary in 2007, which still largely depend on tankers and borewells.

Not exhaustive — a handful of the largest, currently active, publicly documented programmes as of September 2026. Budgets, timelines and scope shift often; each entry is dated to its source.

  • Cauvery Water Supply Scheme, Stage 5775 MLD total (Phase 1: 500 MLD, Phase 2: 275 MLD)
    Extends piped Cauvery water to the 110 peripheral villages (~225 sq km) added to the city in 2007.
  • Underground drainage (UGD) extension14 new STPs, ~124 MLD capacity
    New sewerage network and treatment plants for the same 110-village area, including ~7 intermediate sewage pumping stations.
  • Citywide STP capacity expansion+470 MLD across 26 new STPs
    Broader sewage treatment capacity increase beyond the 110-village programme.

Sources: PropNewz, May 2026, Water Digest

Reach The Desk

Where to actually take this

BWSSB's field structure (service station → sub-division → division) is exactly where a site-specific water/sewerage query needs to go, and each level has a functional contact route.

Why no names below the top tier: Assistant Engineers and Executive Engineers rotate across sub-divisions/divisions without a continuously maintained public name list. These links go straight to the agency's own citizen-facing channel instead of a name that may already be out of date.
